Layer-wise congruent-vs-incongruent decoding accuracy (with 95% bootstrap CI bands) for every
base/instruct model pair across the three families with a base checkpoint: Gemma3, Gemma4, and
InternVL3 (columns, left to right). Rows are ordered by model size within each family, smallest
to largest, top to bottom; rows are aligned by size rank, not by matching absolute parameter
count across families, since the three families don't share sizes. Each subplot's x-axis is
layer depth normalized to [0, 1] (0 = first layer, 1 = final layer) so models with different
layer counts overlay comparably; the size label in each subplot's lower-right corner identifies
that row's model within its family. The solid green line is the instruction-tuned checkpoint and
the dashed orange line is its base counterpart; the gray dashed horizontal line marks the
permutation-based significance threshold (~0.58).

Across all three families and nearly every size, decoding accuracy rises sharply from
near-threshold in the earliest layers to a peak (typically >0.9) around the middle-to-late
layers, then often declines somewhat toward the final layer. Instruct checkpoints match or
modestly exceed their base counterparts almost everywhere; the gap is most visible in Gemma3,
especially at 12B and 27B, where the instruct model sustains high decoding accuracy through the
later layers while its base counterpart decays more sharply. Gemma4 and InternVL3 instruct/base
curves are nearly overlapping at most sizes, suggesting instruction-tuning has little effect on
where or how strongly picture-sentence congruency is linearly decodable from those two families'
hidden states.
